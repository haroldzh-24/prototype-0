# Phase 8C: local body pose and movement suggestions

## Native stack and extraction

The inspected app uses Expo 57, React Native 0.86, expo-video, local document-picker/file-system video storage, and the Phase 8B local TrainingAudio module. The separate local `TrainingPose` Expo module uses AVAssetImageGenerator and VNDetectHumanBodyPoseRequest revision 1. No new npm dependency, permission, cloud service, camera access or microphone access is required. Expo autolinking resolves the module. Android, web, Expo Go and clients without the rebuilt module report unavailable; existing audio/manual annotation remains available.

References: [Expo 57](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/), [Apple body pose request](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodyposerequest), [AVAssetImageGenerator](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avassetimagegenerator). Swift implementation follows the existing local Expo module layout; native compilation and execution remain unverified on this Windows workspace.

The config requests 10 FPS, at most the first 60 seconds / 600 samples, and frames with a maximum 640-pixel dimension. Native ceilings enforce these bounds independently. Frames are requested in chronological order, one image/Vision request at a time inside autorelease pools. No raw frames are retained. The image generator may decode intervening codec frames internally. The bridge returns bounded normalized observations (up to four people per frame), not pixel buffers.

The generator applies preferredTrackTransform, including rotation and identifiable reflection, before Vision sees an upright image. Vision's lower-left coordinates are converted to top-left normalized displayed-video coordinates. Anatomical left/right labels are preserved. A mirrored recording with reflection already baked into pixels has no recoverable camera-origin flag; its displayed pixels still align with extraction. Returned actual presentation timestamps, rather than requested times, are authoritative video-start milliseconds. Duplicate frame timestamps are discarded. Sampling/tolerance and smoothing introduce uncertainty on the order of 100–200 ms; millisecond coordinates do not imply millisecond detection accuracy.

Progress is polled every 500 ms. Cancellation is registered synchronously before native work is queued and checked between frames; an in-flight frame/Vision call must return first. Editor close discards late results. Audio and pose analyses have separate buttons and run one at a time, keeping marker edits usable. Save/profile contribution waits for analysis to finish.

## Samples, continuity and motion

PoseFrame contains timestamp, displayed width/height, reflection metadata and detected people. Bodies contain detection confidence and optional normalized nose/neck, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle joints with individual confidence. Low-confidence joints are omitted, never fabricated. Selected PoseSamples remain separate from smoothed features.

Initial selection requires one usable torso. Later selection requires a uniquely nearest torso within the continuity threshold; multiple people warn and lower confidence. A long tracking loss (>250 ms) prevents automatic reacquisition for the remainder of that run. This is conservative continuity, not person recognition; similar people crossing can still confuse it. Brief occlusion also breaks motion intervals without interpolation. Full torso landmarks are required for gross movement; partial framing can yield no usable samples.

Exponential smoothing uses actual elapsed time and a centralized 100 ms window. Features include torso-center velocity in normalized video widths/heights per second, relative wrist/ankle articulation, and relative acceleration. They are never Stage Builder coordinates or physical speed. Sustained translation with little articulation is marked camera-ambiguous and downgraded. This does not provide camera-motion compensation, world-space movement, optical flow, or tracking through cuts. Camera movement can still resemble articulated movement.

Movement begins above 0.12 normalized units/second for two nominal samples and ends below 0.045 for 300 ms. Hysteresis prevents threshold toggling. A completed segment needs 0.035 net normalized displacement. Incomplete runs and missing-data interruptions warn rather than invent a stop. Position exit/entry require at least 0.08 net displacement, 400 ms duration, stabilization and confidence above LOW with no camera ambiguity. These are temporal departures/arrivals, not known stage locations. Returning to the same image position, panning cameras or unfavorable angles can prevent detection.

Segment diagnostics store relative peak velocity, acceleration/deceleration onset and stabilization time. Reaction requires a quiet 200 ms pre-stimulus baseline with usable wrists, then sustained torso/relative-wrist motion within two seconds and before the next stimulus. Candidate stimuli produce LOW-confidence reaction suggestions. Ambiguous or absent baseline produces no reaction. Gross arm onset/stabilization is diagnostic only: no HAND_ON_GUN, firearm, precise presentation-complete or reload event is inferred.

Confidence considers joint/body confidence, continuous support, missing fraction, subject ambiguity and camera ambiguity. HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW are heuristics, not probabilities or confirmation. All thresholds are centralized in POSE_ANALYSIS_CONFIG.

## Review, measurements and storage

The existing timeline distinguishes purple pose suggestions and amber audio suggestions. Both share preview/edit/confirm/delete. Segment bars show current derived intervals and clearly labeled last-run diagnostics. Editing machine events revokes confirmation; old detector provenance remains available. An optional skeleton overlay renders only observed saved joints, with proper contain/letterbox geometry; it hides outside a 100 ms proximity to a saved sample. “Preview next saved pose sample” supports sparse overlay review. No interpolation across missing samples and no overlay inside native full-screen playback.

Manual/confirmed events of the same type within 100 ms suppress duplicate suggestions and record a match. Reruns replace only unconfirmed POSE_DETECTED markers, preserving manual, confirmed and audio events. Interpretation uses current stimuli and markers, including changes made during extraction. Rejected suggestions can return on explicit rerun; no rejection blacklist is inferred.

The existing analyzeVideo engine handles confirmed REACTION, MOVEMENT_START/STOP and POSITION_EXIT/ENTRY. Unconfirmed pose events cannot alter trusted intervals or split shooting strings. Confirmed reaction becomes the existing stimulusResponseTime observation. Movement speed is eligible only with positive known physical distance on MOVEMENT_START; normalized displacement is never substituted. Profile rebuild still requires the existing explicit contribution action. Position-transition duration remains diagnostic to avoid double-counting the paired movement interval.

Optional poseRun extends analysis version 1 compatibly, retaining its own detector/config version, native Vision revision, run timestamp, bounded original candidates/segments, arm diagnostics, warnings, match records and at most 120 selected raw preview samples. No full raw sample history, smoothed arrays or video frames enter SQLite. Persistence validation reconstructs known fields and rejects unsupported versions. Last-run diagnostics are not the current review state; timeline markers remain authoritative. Preview data may be stale relative to later manual marker edits and is labeled as run evidence.
